Question 3 of 4 · Required

What is your country of chargeability?

Normally this is your country of birth. See the note below for the spouse exception.

Spouse born in a different country?
Under INA §202(b) you may be able to charge your case to your spouse's country of birth if it has a shorter queue. Requirements are strict (both spouses as beneficiaries, marriage exists when a visa becomes available). Ask an immigration attorney before assuming this applies.
Question 4 of 4 · Required

What is your priority date?

Your priority date depends on your category: for a PERM-based case (most EB-2 and EB-3) it is the day the Department of Labor received your ETA-9089 labor certification; for EB-1 and the EB-2 National Interest Waiver, which skip PERM, it is the day USCIS received your I-140 petition. Optional

Are you changing roles?

Optional. What matters legally is whether your duties change materially, not your title or level. A same-track promotion usually carries lower PERM risk; a move into management or a different job family more often needs reassessment. It depends on your actual duties, so confirm with an attorney.

Where do you currently work, and where might you consider moving?

Same MSA usually means lower risk of a PERM location problem; a different MSA is usually treated as a material change and often requires reassessment. The MSA (not the state line) is the test, but the actual impact depends on your case stage and whether duties or wage also change. Confirm with an immigration attorney.
