Government filing fees are the same across all six paths. They go to USCIS and are separate from any attorney fee. Form I-140 (the immigrant petition) is $715 by mail or $665 online. A separate Asylum Program Fee is due with the I-140 and is not part of it: $300 for a self-petitioner, $600 if an employer files as a regular petitioner, $0 for a nonprofit. Form I-907 (optional premium processing of the I-140) is $2,965, paid on top of the I-140 fee. It buys a decision within the posted business-day window and does not shorten the visa-bulletin wait, so confirm the current amount on the official I-907 page. Form I-485 (adjustment of status inside the U.S.) is $1,440 by mail or $1,390 online. What differs between paths is who usually pays, shown in the cost row.

Queue positions are directional, not predictions. Backlogs are charged to your country of birth and move month to month. To see the actual movement, use this site's History and Trends for how each category's cutoff has moved over the years, PERM Processing Time for how long the labor-certification step has taken, and the Queue Position and Timeline Scenarios tool to explore a range from your own dates. For the official current figures, check the USCIS processing-times page and the monthly Visa Bulletin.

The fit notes are trade-offs, not a recommendation. None of this decides your case. Which path is realistic for you depends on the specific facts of your background and your employer, so confirm with a licensed immigration attorney. You can find one through the American Immigration Lawyers Association.

O-1 is not included here. It is a temporary work visa, not one of the six green card paths, so it is left out of this comparison. The EB-2 NIW page has a narrative table that includes O-1 for context.
